Nonlinear, 2026, Website

Non-internet is a website of 15 HTML pages, all coded by me. It opens on a collage of broken desktop windows and toolbars, then a login screen drawn as an ASCII computer where any username and password works. From there, the layout is nonlinear. A gray hallway acts as a hub, and the other pages branch off it. You can find a face scan that follows your cursor, a chatroom where nobody answers, a scrolling wall of nature images, and a "Friend Finder" that asks you to accept or reject blurry faces. Some pages pick up what you type on others. Whatever you enter at "Identify Self" comes back on the next page, and your chat messages reappear as ASCII text.

I developed it over the course of a month, and it’s about the parts of the Internet that feel most familiar to me: logging in as nobody, scrolling past strangers, judging faces, and being asked who you are by a text box. The pages work like rooms you wander through rather than steps in a path, so the experience reflects how the Internet feels more than how it works.
